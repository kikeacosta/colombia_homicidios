Missing deaths in the Caribbean

The pre-2000 anomaly in the sex ratio–homicide relationship is not a cause-coding problem. In six of the seven Caribbean departments, between half and four in five adult deaths went unregistered in 1985–95. The gap starts to close in 1998.

01

What this found

You asked whether homicides were being hidden in other cause categories before 2000, and why the effect concentrated in the Caribbean coast. The answer is that the deaths are not miscoded — they are absent.

In 1985, registered crude death rates in La Guajira, Córdoba and Sucre were 1.7–2.2 per 1,000, against 6.0–6.3 in Antioquia, Bogotá and Santander. Colombia’s age structure cannot produce rates that low. A generalized growth balance estimate — which uses no cause-of-death information at all — puts adult registration completeness at 0.20–0.51 in six Caribbean departments for 1985–95, and 0.33–0.96 by 2005–15 — with La Guajira still below one half.

The all-cause sex ratio you are using as a homicide signal is therefore a ratio of two differently incomplete counts, and the completeness differential between men and women swings between 0.5 and 1.45 across departments and years — the same order of magnitude as the signal itself. The step change lands in 1998, with DANE's new death certificate, not in 2000.

02

Diagnosis

Four tests, run against your own data. The first rules out your working hypothesis; the rest identify what is actually happening.

The hidden-homicide hypothesis does not survive

The timing looked right. Ill-defined causes (list 105 code 89) collapse from 21.5% of Caribbean deaths in 1985 to 2.1% in 1998 — exactly at the ICD-10 switch. If homicides had been hiding in that bucket, it would explain the break.

But the sex ratio of the ill-defined deaths themselves settles the question. Homicides run at 11 to 13 male deaths per female death. Ill-defined deaths run at 1.08–1.33 — close to natural causes (1.05–1.08), and nowhere near homicide.

Sex ratio (male deaths ÷ female deaths) by cause group, 1985–2017. If ill-defined deaths were concealed homicides they would sit near 13, not near 1.1. Treating the bucket as a mixture implies only about 2% of it behaves like homicide.

Redistributing ill-defined deaths pro rata across known causes makes the sex ratio–homicide correlations no better, and slightly worse. Adding the ill-defined share to a fixed-effects model moves R² from 0.773 to 0.783, and its coefficient is negative — more ill-defined coding goes with a lower sex ratio.

The registered death rates are implausibly low

This is the test that settles it, and it needs no cause coding, no model and no assumptions beyond arithmetic. A crude death rate is bounded below by the population’s age structure. The only populations that register rates this low are made up largely of young migrant workers, such as the Gulf states; Colombia’s own best-registered departments sit at 5–6.

Completeness, estimated independently

Generalized growth balance (Hill 1987) recovers registration completeness from the balance between entries into an age group, the group's growth rate, and deaths out of it. It uses population and deaths by age only — no causes, so it cannot be contaminated by the coding question above.

GGB completeness of adult death registration, ages 15–64, fitted on 10-year windows. Values below 1 mean under-registration. The six low-coverage Caribbean departments move by 0.06 or less across three alternative fitting age ranges; Tolima and Cundinamarca move by up to 0.21, so read those with more caution (data_inter/q_test/completeness_ggb_sensitivity.csv).

Does the correction hold up?

The check that matters is external. Colombia's registered national crude death rate is flat at 4.4–5.1 per 1,000 across the whole period — too low for the country's age structure, and implausibly trendless. Dividing through by the estimated completeness should land it in a demographically sensible range without being told to.

What this does to your correlation

Once the measurement story is clear, the correlation between the log sex ratio and the log homicide rate is best read as a data-quality gradient rather than a Caribbean phenomenon. The relationship strengthens after 1998 almost everywhere.

Two further results worth carrying forward. A breakpoint scan over 1988–2010 peaks at 1995 with R² spanning only 0.773–0.788 across the whole range — there is no sharp structural break, just a gradual improvement in data quality. And in first differences — does a department's year-to-year change in sex ratio track its change in homicide rate? — the pre-1998 correlation is 0.11 outside the Caribbean and 0.25 inside it, against 0.43 and 0.58 after. Almost all of the apparent predictive power is cross-sectional and trend-driven, not annual.

code/01_loading_microdata.Rbug fix

Age groups were shifted by one five-year band for 1979–1997. The recode read:

gr_edad %in% 8:24 ~ (gr_edad - 8) * 5
gr_edad %in% 8:24 ~ (gr_edad - 7) * 5

The DANE dictionary in documents/metadata/1979-1991.pdf defines GRU_ED1 as 08 = de 5 a 9 años, 09 = de 10 a 14, 10 = de 15 a 19 … 24 = de 85 y más. So group 8 — true ages 5–9 — was being assigned age 0, and everything from 10–14 upward was aged down five years. The 1998–2007 block was already correct, which means the two eras disagreed with each other across exactly the period under investigation. The age-0 share of deaths in 1985 drops from 13.3% to 11.8% once corrected.

Also fixed the file glob: "\\.txt$||\\.csv$" contains an empty alternative and matches every file in the directory. Harmless with today's 45 files, a trap later.

code/12_registration_completeness.Rnew, 506 lines

Estimates completeness two ways and reconciles them.

  • GGB by department, sex and 10-year window, with the fitted intercept and R² retained as diagnostics.
  • An annual coverage index — the department's age-standardised natural-cause rate relative to the national one — rescaled so its mean over each GGB window reproduces the GGB level, giving a usable annual covariate.
  • A balance diagnostic plot of the GGB fits themselves, because curvature invalidates the estimate and you should look before trusting a number.
  • A sensitivity table over three fitting age ranges.
  • The national CDR sanity check shown above.

It recomputes edad from gr_edad itself, so it runs correctly against the existing micro_1979_2023.rds without waiting on a re-run of 01.

Judgement calls worth knowing about

  • Completeness is capped at 1. The cap binds for Bogotá, Caldas, Quindío, Risaralda and Tolima, which show as flat lines at 1.0.
  • The six micro-departments — Guainía, Vaupés, Vichada, Amazonas, Guaviare, San Andrés — have single-digit annual natural-cause death counts, so GGB is meaningless there. They get reliable = FALSE and completeness = NA rather than a fabricated number.
  • The annual index assumes differences in natural-cause mortality between departments are small relative to differences in coverage. That is the weak link and it is flagged in the script header.

04

Remove the two predictors that cannot exist historically high value

cdr_std is min–max scaled within municipality across the full 1985–2017 window, so it uses the future to define each year's value and cannot be computed for 1930. exc_mx_log is worse: excess deaths are derived from dx_tot, which contains the homicides being predicted. The tiny AIC gain is consistent with it adding little beyond that circularity.

Note the AIC ladders in the comments of 08 and 08_exc compare models fitted to different row counts and are not comparable.

05

Validate the way the model will actually be used high value

Predictions are raked to observed departmental totals at 08:106 and then scored against the same observed data in 09. The department total is guaranteed to match, so only the within-department allocation is being tested. Two cheap tests fix this: a temporal backcast fitting on 2000–2017 and predicting 1985–1999, which mimics the real use; and leave-department-out, raking to a held-out total and scoring the municipal shares.

Also pred ± 1.96·se.fit is an interval for the conditional mean, ignoring negative-binomial dispersion and the zero-inflation component. Use simulate() on the fitted object instead.

06

Replace the sex ratio with a targeted signal method

Homicide is overwhelmingly young and male, so the male–female difference in age-specific rates at ages 15–44 carries the signal while ages 60+ add noise and reflect coverage differences rather than violence. That difference is an absolute excess-male-mortality rate: on the same scale as a homicide rate, stable when denominators are small, and needing no standardisation. It also removes the need for the (dx_tot + 5) hack at 06:98, which inflates a 10-death municipality by 50%.

And drop min–max standardisation everywhere — it is not scale-free, not comparable across departments, and undefined out of sample.

07

Decompose the change, not the level method

The algebra in 11 is a valid exact decomposition of R − 1, but it decomposes each year separately and leaves you eyeballing 33 time series. Your question is about a change, so decompose the difference between two periods directly — Kitagawa or Das Gupta, or Horiuchi–Wilmoth–Pletcher if you want age and cause simultaneously. That gives one number per cause for “how much of the 1995→2005 change does this account for”.

Fix alongside cont_per explodes when the ratio approaches 1, which is why the plot needs clamping; cont_pos_per discards negative contributions and no longer sums to the decomposed quantity; and smooth_conts() fits a proportion in [0,1] with family = "quasipoisson". Report cont in absolute units, and add missing deaths as an explicit component — before 1998 in the Caribbean it will dominate every cause in the table.

08

Reconcile the historical series with the completeness model method

This is the one that decides whether the 1920s–1979 estimates mean anything. You are calibrating on 1985–2017, where Caribbean completeness starts at 20–50%, and extrapolating to 1930, where it was certainly worse and varied in ways you cannot observe. The relationship you estimate is conditional on a registration regime that did not exist historically. 20_historic_data_coverage.R already documents the annuary coverage gaps; fuse that with the completeness estimates into one explicit measurement-error model rather than treating the historical counts as directly comparable.

Reproducibility gap nothing in code/ generates who_homicide_colombia.rds or who_all_cause_colombia.rds, which 20 and 22 both depend on.

05

What this does not establish

Three things I could not settle, stated plainly so they do not get quoted as findings.

GGB assumes no net migration

caveat

In departments with heavy displacement or male out-migration, emigrants look like missing deaths and male completeness is biased downwards. Antioquia 1985–95 is the clear case: male completeness 0.73 against female 1.10, in the epicentre of the violence. The Caribbean estimates are less exposed to this, but not immune.

This cuts both ways for the project. If DANE's projections overstate the male 15–64 population in violent municipalities because displacement is under-captured, the denominator of your homicide rate is too large there — a second measurement problem pushing in the same direction as the first.

The annual index is the weak link

caveat

Genuine excess natural mortality is read as better coverage. The GGB levels are defensible on their own; the annual interpolation between them rests on an assumption that is convenient rather than demonstrated. The cap at 1 binds for five departments, which is where you would see this if it were biting hard.

Completeness is estimated relative to DANE's projections

scope

The denominators here are projected populations, not census counts, so what is measured is completeness relative to DANE's own population series. That is the right quantity for this project — the same denominators build the rates — but it is not the same as completeness against truth, and it should not be quoted as such.

For a fuller treatment — SEG, the GGB-SEG hybrid, and proper age-trimming diagnostics — the DDM package implements all three and would be a sensible upgrade path from the single-estimator version in 12.
