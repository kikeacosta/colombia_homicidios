# Census summary tables 1938, 1951, 1964 and 1973 (DANE PDFs)

Tables extracted from the four census summary volumes kept in
`data_input/historical/` (PDFs not versioned, 35 MB) by
`code/historical/23_census_tables_ocr.R` (the two `census_1973_muestra_*` files come from the muestra de avance, `code/historical/27_censo_1973_muestra_avance.R`, see below), which also re-checks every
accounting identity listed below and stops if one fails. All counts are
persons as printed by DANE; nothing is smoothed or adjusted here.

## Sources (pdf page numbers of the files in `data_input/historical/`)

- **1938**: `LD_771_1938_V_1.pdf`, *Censo general de población, 5 de julio de 1938, resumen general del país*, Cuadro 28 "Población clasificada por edades, sexo y estado civil", pp. 105-107. Only the "totales" block is used (the solteros / casados / viudos blocks were not validated). Grand totals 4,312,763 men, 4,389,053 women, 8,701,816.
- **1951**: `LD_771_1951.pdf`, *Censo de población de 1951, resumen*, Cuadro 10 "Población por grupos de edad y sexo, 1938 y 1951, por departamentos, intendencias y comisarías", pp. 22-32. Enumerated population 11,228,509 in 1951 (DANE's later adjusted figure, 11,548,172, is not in this table) and 8,697,041 in 1938. The 1938 column excludes the 4,775 `indígena` persons of Cuadro 28 (2,606 men, 2,169 women), which is exactly the difference with the 1938 grand total. Vichada has no 1951 data (dashes) and is left empty.
- **1964**: `LD_771_1964.pdf`, *XIII Censo nacional de población (julio 15 de 1964), resumen general*. Cuadro 7 "Población por sexo, cabeceras y resto de los municipios, según grupos de edad (departamentos y territorios nacionales)", pp. 26-32, and Cuadro 9 "Población del país por sexo, cabeceras y resto de los municipios, según años simples de edad", pp. 35-36. Enumerated population 17,484,508 (8,614,652 men, 8,869,856 women), 9,093,094 in cabeceras. Territorial division of 1964: Meta is already a departamento, La Guajira still an intendencia, Putumayo a comisaría, and Cesar, Quindío, Risaralda and Sucre do not exist yet. Dashes in the small comisarías are zeros. Cuadro 14 "Población nacida en el país y en el extranjero por lugar de nacimiento y sexo, según lugar de residencia y grupos de edad", pp. 49-61 and 63 (p. 62 is a chart): residence in rows, ten units per half-page block (pp. 49, 50, 53, 54, 57, 58, 61 for the country, Antioquia … Cundinamarca; pp. 51, 52, 55, 56, 59, 60, 63 for Chocó … Valle and the intendencias and comisarías), four lines each (total, under 15, 15-59, 60 and over); birthplace in columns, three groups of total / men / women per page. In the national row 74,053 persons were born abroad, and, among the residents of the 17 departamentos and D.E. who were born in one of them, 82.2 % live in the one where they were born.
- **1973**: `LB_771_1973.pdf`, *XIV Censo nacional de población y III de vivienda, octubre 24 de 1973, resumen nacional*. Cuadro 2 "Población censada por grupos de edad y sexo, total, cabecera y resto", pp. 316-317 (only the national columns are transcribed; censused total 20,666,920). Chapter VIII "Ajuste por cobertura de la población", pp. 115-197 (TOTAL / CABECERA / RESTO rows of each sección; the municipal rows are not transcribed) and Cuadro VIII.20, p. 94 (national summary: adjusted population 22,915,229 = 22,487,118 in departamentos and D.E. + 375,000 in intendencias and comisarías + 53,111 fuerzas armadas; the implied omission is 9.8 %). Cuadro 21 "Población femenina de 15 años y más en hogares particulares y colectivos por número de hijos nacidos vivos y sobrevivientes a la fecha del censo, según grupos de edad", p. 381, and Cuadro 21A, the same by secciones, pp. 382-384. They cover 5,937,170 women against 6,020,982 women aged 15+ in Cuadro 2, because areas with indigenous population are outside this tabulation (hence 86 women in Vichada and 310 in Guainía), and they are censused counts, not adjusted for coverage. 18.5 % of the women did not state their number of children (40 % at 15-19), so parities need an El-Badry type correction before use; the national proportion of children dead rises from 0.088 at 20-24 to 0.165 at 45-49.
- **1973 muestra de avance**: `data_input/dane_pdfs/LD_1007_1973_EJ_4.pdf` (not versioned), DANE, *La población en Colombia 1973. XIV Censo Nacional de Población y III de Vivienda, muestra de avance. Resumen por departamentos, capitales de más de 200 000 habitantes y agregados*, read by `code/historical/27_censo_1973_muestra_avance.R`. Cuadros 1 (5-year age groups by sex and zone) and 2 (single ages by sex) of the 23 sections added, pp. 40-41; for each of the 22 departamentos and Bogotá D.E., in the order of the sections (Antioquia, Atlántico, Bogotá D.E., Bolívar, Boyacá, Caldas, Cauca, Cesar, Córdoba, Chocó, Cundinamarca, Huila, La Guajira, Magdalena, Meta, Nariño, Norte de Santander, Quindío, Risaralda, Santander, Sucre, Tolima, Valle del Cauca), Cuadro 1 "Población por sexo, según grupos de edad" on pp. 72 + 11 k and Cuadro 2 "Población por sexo, según edades simples" on pp. 73 + 11 k, k = 0 … 22 (the department is the large heading of the first page of its section). **These are sample-expanded figures, not the final census count**: the muestra de avance took one private household in 25 and expanded the persons by 25 (by 28 in Bogotá D.E., adjusted with the post-census coverage survey), excluding collective households, floating population, armed forces, the indigenous population and the territorios nacionales (no intendencia or comisaría appears). The 23 sections total 19,735,286 persons (9,587,026 men, 10,148,260 women) against 22,487,118 coverage-adjusted for the same units (87.8 %; from 98.2 % in Bogotá D.E. to 51.2 % in La Guajira) and 20,666,920 censused in the whole country. Every cell is a multiple of 25 (28 in Bogotá), and the publication puts the sampling error at about 10 % for 12,000 persons and 25 % or more below 1,500, so single-age cells of the small departments are noisy. The same pages also print Cuadros 3 and later (literacy, schooling, activity, children ever born), not extracted; the 9 city sections (pp. 326-428) are not extracted either.

## Method

- **1951** (OCR): each page rendered at 500 dpi (grey, thresholded) and at 300 dpi; tesseract 5 (page mode 6) word boxes clustered into rows and into the six rightmost numeric columns; tokens split at a thousands separator merged; low-confidence or malformed cells re-read one by one (page mode 7, digit whitelist). The two passes are reconciled with the identities total = men + women (every row), 21 age rows = section header, and departamentos + intendencias + comisarías = nación. 190 cells that no identity could settle were read visually from page crops and are listed in `ocr_support/corrections_1951.csv`. The grid output of both passes is in `ocr_support/ocr_grid_1951_*.csv`, so the table is rebuilt without the PDF; `run_ocr <- TRUE` in the script repeats the OCR.
- **1964** (OCR): same grid OCR as 1951 with two changes, because the table is denser (line pitch about 1.1 times the digit height) and its typeface is misread more often (5/6, 3/8, 7/1). Rows are detected from the baseline of the numeric tokens on the deskewed page, and five passes are run (300, 400, 500 and 600 dpi grey, 500 dpi thresholded; `ocr_support/ocr_grid_1964_*.csv`). Each printed row has nine cells, (total, cabecera, resto) × (total, men, women), tied by six identities. A row is the combination of readings that satisfies all six, or the majority reading with one or two cells replaced by what the identities imply: of the 661 rows, 425 were settled directly, 180 with one cell and 17 with two cells implied, 5 as the residual of their section, and 34 were read by eye from page crops (`ocr_support/corrections_1964_rows.csv`).
- **1964 Cuadro 14** (OCR plus eye): three passes (300 and 400 dpi grey, 500 dpi thresholded; `ocr_support/ocr_grid_1964_c14_*.csv`) on the deskewed pages, the right-hand pages (50 … 60) cropped to 76.5 % of the width because they repeat the row labels. A (residence, birthplace) block is four lines × (total, men, women) = 12 cells tied by seven identities (total = men + women in each line; the total line = the three age lines in each column), 420 blocks in all. As for Cuadro 7, a block is the combination of readings that satisfies the seven identities; of the 420: 221 settled directly, 119 with one cell and 17 with two cells implied, 5 as the residual of a row or column of the matrix, 8 `Totales` blocks taken from Cuadro 7 (the OCR of the other 12 `Totales` blocks agrees with it exactly), and 50 read by eye from 400 dpi page crops (`ocr_support/corrections_1964_cuadro14.csv`: residence, birthplace, age, t, h, m). The 3/8 and 5/6 pairs are hard to tell apart even by eye in this typeface, so every digit of those blocks was settled with the identities, not by sight alone.
- **1938 and 1973** (visual transcription): tesseract could not read the 12-column typeset table of 1938 reliably, nor the chain-printer scan of 1973 in any preprocessing variant (6/E, 0/C, 9/S confusions). Both were transcribed by eye from 500 dpi crops (`ocr_support/transcription_*.csv`) and corrected until every identity held.
- **1973 muestra de avance** (text layer plus identities plus eye): the library scan has a good text layer, so no OCR was run. Word boxes are cached (`data_inter/`); the right edges of the numeric tokens form the columns and the vertical centres the lines. Cuadro 2 prints the single ages in two groups of columns side by side, both ending on the last line (`85 y más`), and the split between them varies by section (ages 0-41 and 42-85+ in 4 pages, 0-42 and 43-85+ in the other 20), so the first line of the right-hand group fixes the age of each line. The 7,824 cells (2,608 rows of three: per section 20 of Cuadro 1 and 87 of Cuadro 2, plus the national blocks) are tied by 4,579 linear identities (below); a cell of the text layer is accepted only if it is a well formed number. 174 cells were wrong or empty in the text layer (71 misread: a digit taken for another, mostly 5 and 6, 3 and 8, 0 and 6, or a stray or missing digit; 102 garbled or missing, 33 of them printed dashes, i.e. zeros; and one misprint of the print itself): each takes the value that two or more identities ask for (every correction repairs 3 to 5 identities) and was compared with a 300 dpi crop of the page; they are listed in `ocr_support/corrections_1973_muestra.csv` (`table`, `page`, `unit`, `cod_dpto`, `zone`, `age`, `sex`, `text_layer`, `value_read`, `value`, `kind` = `misread` / `unreadable` / `print_misprint`, `note`, `checked`). `find_corrections <- TRUE` in the script proposes the list again from the identities.

## Validation (all asserted by the script)

- 1938: total = men + women in each of the 101 rows; column sums equal the printed grand totals.
- 1951: total = men + women in each of the 29 × 21 × 2 rows; the 21 ages add to every section header in all six columns; departments, intendencias and comisarías add to their group totals and to the national total in all six columns.
- 1964: the six identities (total = men + women in each zone, total = cabecera + resto for each sex) in each of the 661 rows; the 19 age groups add to the header in the nine columns of the 28 sections; the 27 units add to the national section in every age group and column; the single ages of Cuadro 9 add to its header and, grouped, to the national age groups of Cuadro 7; national total 17,484,508.
- 1964 Cuadro 14: the seven identities in each of the 420 blocks; for every residence and line, `Totales` = the sum of the 20 birthplaces in total, men and women; for every birthplace and line, the national row = the sum of the 19 other residences; the `Totales` group = Cuadro 7 (zone total, ages grouped 0-14 / 15-59 / 60+, intendencias and comisarías summed); 20 × 21 × 4 rows with no gaps. Nothing in the printed volume failed these checks.
- 1973 Cuadro 2: total = men + women in each row; the single ages add to each of the 20 printed 5-year rows and to the printed grand total.
- 1973 chapter VIII: total = particulares + colectivos + indígenas and total = cabecera + resto in every unit; the 24 departamentos + D.E. and the 8 intendencias + comisarías add to Cuadro VIII.20 in all four columns.
- 1973 Cuadros 21 and 21A: women = no informan + sin hijos + con hijos in each of the 27 + 93 rows; the eight age groups add to the TOTAL row of each zone; total = cabecera + resto in every age group and every unit; the 31 units add to the national rows of Cuadro 21 in all six columns and three zones.
- 1973 muestra de avance (asserted by `27_censo_1973_muestra_avance.R`): total = men + women in all 2,608 rows (87 of Cuadro 2 and 20 of Cuadro 1 per section, plus the national blocks); the ages add up to the TOTAL row, in Cuadro 1 and Cuadro 2, for total, men and women; the Cuadro 1 header equals the Cuadro 2 header; every Cuadro 1 age group equals the sum of its single ages in Cuadro 2 (23 sections × 19 groups × 3); in the national block total = cabecera + resto in 20 rows × 3; and the 23 sections added equal the national Cuadros 1 and 2 of pp. 40-41 in every single age, age group and sex. All hold except those that the one misprint of the print breaks: Boyacá, Cuadro 2, age 56, total, is printed 5.550 beside 2.475 men and 2.075 women (4.550), and the age rows, the 55-59 group of Cuadro 1 and the national table all ask for 4,550. The four identities that fail in the print are listed in `ocr_support/source_inconsistencies_1973_muestra.csv` (the script stops if the failing set changes) and the corrected value is the one in the csv files. Every population cell of a department is a multiple of 25 (28 in Bogotá D.E.). The masculinity index printed in Cuadro 1 agrees with 100 men / women to two decimals in all 510 readable cells but three (text-layer misreads of a check column).
- 1973 muestra against the national table of the resumen (`ocr_support/compare_1973_census_vs_muestra_single_age.csv`, single ages 0-84 and 85+, with differences and ratios by sex): `census1973_single_age_sex.csv` is the complete count of the whole country (20,666,920), the muestra covers 19,735,286 (95.5 %; −931,634, of which −537,368 men and −394,266 women). By 5-year group the muestra minus the census is −10.4 % under 1, −5.0 % at 1-4, −4.1 % at 5-9, −3.9 % at 10-14, −5.1 % at 15-19, −5.9 % at 20-24, −4.1 % at 25-29, −4.8 % at 30-34, −3.6 %, −3.9 %, −3.0 %, −2.9 % at 35-54, −2.2 % at 55-59, −3.3 % at 60-64, −2.0 % at 65-69, −4.0 % at 70-74, −6.2 %, −6.7 % and −7.1 % at 75-79, 80-84 and 85+. The ratio of the single ages is about 0.955 at 5-19 and 0.944 at 20-59, 0.96 above 60, with age 0 at 0.896 (466,300 against 520,489); the share of the ages ending in 0 or 5 among 30-79 is the same, 32.3 % against 32.6 %. The gap is therefore coverage (territories, armed forces, indigenous and collective households, and a smaller infant count), not an age pattern of the sample, but infants and the oldest ages are the weakest cells.

## Not extracted

- 1973 Cuadro 2 by zone (cabecera / resto columns). The resumen volume has no age table by department: its Cuadro 2 is national only (the departmental age tables are the 1973 muestra de avance above), and Cuadro 3 (marital status by 5-year age and sex, pp. 318-319) is national by zone.
- 1973 chapter VIII at the municipal level (about 1,000 rows; OCR unusable).
- 1973 Cuadros 22, 22A and 23 (pp. 385-389): women by number of children ever born (0, 1, … 7+) by education, sección and marital status. They have no survivors and no age, so they add nothing for mortality.
- Other 1973 and 1951 tables of possible use: population by sex and municipality 1973 (Cuadro 1A, pp. 264-315), place of birth by place of enumeration (1973 Cuadro 4A, pp. 326-349; 1951 Cuadro 15), 5-year age by sex, zone and department 1951 (Cuadro 11, pp. 37-46).
- 1938 Cuadro 28 marital-status blocks and Cuadro 29 (departments by broad age groups), for which only unvalidated OCR drafts were produced.
- 1964: Cuadro 13 (born in the same department / in other departments / abroad, by unit of residence, sex and zone, pp. 45-48; it would check the diagonal of Cuadro 14); Cuadro 1 (country by age, sex and zone in 1951 and 1964); Cuadros 10-11 (marital status) and 18-22 (literacy and education). The volume has no table of children ever born.
